## 1. Inputs and Timing

* **Why do encoder signals pass through a synchronizer, and what goes wrong without one?** Asynchronous encoder edges violate setup and hold timing on `quad_decoder`'s `ab_previous` flip-flops, causing metastability and random position jumps. In `synchronizer_subsystem.sv` (lines 13–14), `ENCODER_A_SYNC` and `ENCODER_B_SYNC` condition `ch_a` and `ch_b` first.
* **Why do the synchronizer flip-flops have no reset?** No reset lets the synchronizer synchronize the reset button itself (`RESET_SYNC`). Any unknown power-up state flushes within two clock cycles (`synchronizer.sv` lines 7–8).
* **What is `sample_tick`, where does it come from, and why does the controller only update on it?** `sample_tick` is a one-cycle pulse at 100 Hz generated by `downcounter_subsystem` (`CONTROL_TICK_GEN` with `PERIOD = 1,000,000` in `downcounter_subsystem.sv` line 4 and `downcounter.sv`). In `p_controller.sv` (line 25), `if (sample_tick)` gates `p_action` updates so the controller runs at a deterministic 100 Hz sample rate (10 ms period).
* **Why is it a clock enable, not a second clock?** Keeping every `always_ff` on `posedge clk` (100 MHz) maintains a single clock domain—avoiding CDC, multi-clock static timing analysis, and extra floorplanning complexity.

## 2. The Decoder

* **A and B waveforms for one line forward, and count yield:** Turning forward (A leads B by 90°), the state sequence is `00 -> 10 -> 11 -> 01 -> 00`. Four edges per cycle yield **4 counts per encoder line** (quadrature x4 decoding), giving $4 \times 512 = 2048$ counts per revolution (`quad_decoder.sv` header and lines 13–14).
  ```text
  clk / cycle:   |  00  |  10  |  11  |  01  |  00  |
  ch_a (lead):   |__    |^^^^^^|^^^^^^|____  |__    |
  ch_b (lag):    |____  |____  |^^^^^^|^^^^^^|____  |
  ```
* **Both A and B change in the same clock cycle:** Illegal simultaneous transitions (`00_11`, `10_01`, `11_00`, `01_10`) fall into `default: position <= position` (`quad_decoder.sv` line 15) and are ignored. At 100 MHz (10 ns period), a real encoder cannot change both lines in one cycle.
* **Swapping A and B:** Reverses the state ring so forward transitions become backward transitions and vice versa (`quad_decoder.sv` lines 13–14). Fix it on the hardware by swapping the two motor leads on L298N `OUT3`/`OUT4` with no HDL change.

## 3. The Counts Display

* **Negative position to 0–2047:** `position_to_degrees.sv` (line 10) takes `count_in_rev = position[10:0]` (lower 11 bits). Under two's complement, `-512` (`16'hFE00`) has lower 11 bits equal to `1536` ($2048 - 512$), which maps cleanly into `0..2047` (and corresponds to $270^\circ$, or $-90^\circ \pmod{360^\circ}$).
* **Why only for a power of two (2048)?** Extracting a bit slice `position[10:0]` computes modulo $2^{11} = 2048$. For a non-power-of-two count like 2000, bit slicing does not equal modulo 2000, and shifting by 11 bits does not divide by 2000.
* **Changes for a 500-line encoder:** `ENCODER_LINES = 500` gives `COUNTS_PER_REV = 2000` (`disk_pkg.sv`). `REV_BITS` stays 11 ($2^{11} = 2048 \ge 2000$), but `position_to_degrees.sv` cannot use a simple power-of-two mask/shift for exact modulo 2000 and scaling by 360/2000—it requires modulo/division logic or updated scaling.
* **Four digits in counts mode vs. three in degrees mode:** Raw counts range up to `2047` (4 digits), while degrees range from `0` to `359` (3 digits). In `display_subsystem.sv`, `digit_blank` is `4'b1000` in degrees mode, blanking the leftmost anode (`AN4`) so `359` displays as `" 359"` rather than `"0359"`.

## 4. Arithmetic and Data Types

* **Why `error` is 17 bits from 16-bit inputs:** `error = reference - measured` (`p_controller.sv` line 19). With 16-bit signed inputs (`-32,768` to `+32,767`), the largest possible difference is $32,767 - (-32,768) = 65,535$, which requires 17 bits signed to avoid overflow.
* **What `$signed({1'b0, kp})` does, and what breaks without it:** `kp` is a 4-bit unsigned value (`0..15`). Prepending `1'b0` makes a 5-bit unsigned zero-extended value, and `$signed(...)` types it as a positive signed operand for signed multiplication (`p_controller.sv` line 20). Without `$signed(...)`, Verilog/SystemVerilog treats the multiply as unsigned, so a negative `error` wraps into a large positive number.
* **Why compute the product at full width:** `PRODUCT_WIDTH = 22` (`IN_WIDTH + 1 + KP_WIDTH + 1`, `p_controller.sv` line 14). The maximum product is $65,535 \times 15 = 983,025$ (under $2^{20}$). Truncating early risks overflow and sign inversion before scaling/limiting.
* **How `position_to_degrees` handles negative `position`:** Using `position[REV_BITS-1:0]` extracts the two's complement residue modulo 2048 (`-512` becomes `1536`, then `1536 * 360 / 2048 = 270` degrees).

## 5. The Controller

* **Why limits are parameters, not constants:** Parameterizing `OUT_MAX` and `OUT_MIN` (`p_controller.sv` lines 9–12) makes the module reusable across designs—for example, `±255` for the disk, unlimited P-term in a PI controller where saturation is applied after summing with integral action, or `0..100%` for a boost converter.
* **Why `p_controller` does not import `disk_pkg`:** Keeping it free of `disk_pkg` removes project-specific dependencies so `disk_top` (or another top-level design) can instantiate it with custom widths and limits.
* **What `p_action` does between samples:** `p_action` is a registered output updated only when `sample_tick` is asserted (`p_controller.sv` line 25), holding its previous command across the 10 ms (100 Hz) sample interval.
* **If `error = measured - reference`:** The sign of the feedback loop flips from negative to positive feedback. If the disk moves above the setpoint (`measured > reference`), `error > 0` drives `command > 0` in the direction increasing position further, causing runaway instead of convergence.

## 6. The Motor Drive

* **Signed `command` to three L298N signals:** `motor_drive.sv` computes `duty = |command|` (8-bit magnitude) for `MOTOR_PWM` (`pwm.sv`), which drives `motor_en` (connected to L298N `ENB`). Direction outputs are registered as `motor_in1 = arm && (command > 0)` (L298N `IN3`, forward) and `motor_in2 = arm && (command < 0)` (L298N `IN4`, reverse).
* **Coast mode:** Occurs when `motor_en = 0` (`duty = 0` or disarmed), placing the L298N outputs in a high-impedance state so the disk coasts freely rather than actively braking (shorting motor terminals together with `IN3 = IN4 = 0` while enabled).
* **PWM frequency calculation:** With `PWM_TICK_PERIOD = 39` cycles at 100 MHz and a 255-step PWM counter (`0..254`):
  $$f_{\text{PWM}} = \frac{100\text{ MHz}}{39 \times 255} \approx 10.055\text{ kHz} \approx 10.06\text{ kHz}$$
* **When `sw[15]` is down (`arm = 0`):** `MOTOR_PWM` enable is `0` (`motor_en = 0`), and `motor_in1` and `motor_in2` are forced to `0` (`motor_drive.sv` lines 10, 16–17). All three drive outputs go low and the motor does not drive.

## 7. The Hardware

* **Why a 2.2 kΩ resistor in each encoder wire:** Encoder outputs can pull to 5 V, while Basys3 I/O pins are rated for 3.3 V. A 2.2 kΩ series resistor limits current through the FPGA's internal ESD protection/clamp diodes to 3.3 V.
* **Clamp diode current with and without the resistor:**
  * **With 2.2 kΩ** (assuming a ~3.3 V clamped node from a 5 V pull-up): $I \approx \frac{5\text{ V} - 3.3\text{ V}}{2200\ \Omega} \approx 0.77\text{ mA}$ per line.
  * **Without the resistor:** Current is limited only by source/driver impedance and pin parasitics—potentially tens of milliamps, risking latch-up or pin damage.
* **Why all grounds must connect:** Encoder, L298N driver, and Basys3 FPGA need a common 0 V reference so logic thresholds (`0` and `1`) are well-defined and signals do not float or pick up differential ground bounce.
* **Pin 1 orientation on a Pmod connector:** Facing the connector with the key slot on the left, pin 1 is at the **top-right** (marked by a square pad on the PCB). In the design, `ch_a` and `ch_b` use `JB` (`JB1`, `JB2`), and motor control signals use `JC` (`JC1` = `motor_in1`, `JC2` = `motor_in2`, `JC3` = `motor_en`).

## 8. Design Structure

* **Purpose of `disk_pkg` and "single source of truth":** `disk_pkg.sv` centralizes shared parameters (`CLK_FREQ_HZ`, `ENCODER_LINES`, `COUNTS_PER_REV`, `COMMAND_MAX`, etc.). A **single source of truth** means changing a value in one place (such as `ENCODER_LINES`) updates dependent constants consistently without scattered duplicate literals.
* **Subsystem boundaries:** Splitting into `input_conditioning`, `downcounter_subsystem`, `quad_decoder`, `setpoint_control`, `p_controller`, `motor_drive`, and `display_subsystem` groups related functions along clean interfaces (conditioning, timing, sensing, setpoint, control law, actuation, display).
* **What a subsystem hides vs. shows:** It **exposes** clean ports and top-level parameters while **hiding** internal counters, bit-width math, state encoding, and implementation details.
* **Why `disk_top` is structural only:** `disk_top.sv` contains only parameter derivation, signal wires, module instantiations, and LED assignments—no control or state logic. That keeps hierarchy clear, simplifies swapping blocks (such as adding integral action), and lets testbenches override parameters cleanly.

## 9. Control Theory

* **Why high $K_p$ oscillates despite an ideal continuous model:** Ideal first/second-order models omit discrete sampling delay (10 ms at 100 Hz), PWM/encoder quantization, actuator/current dynamics, and mechanical compliance. Added phase lag pushes phase margin down so high $K_p$ produces sustained or growing oscillations.
* **Measuring $K_u$ and $T_u$ on the rig:** Gradually increase $K_p$ with `arm` enabled until the disk exhibits sustained, constant-amplitude oscillations; that gain is the ultimate gain $K_u$, and the period of one full oscillation cycle on a scope/trace is the ultimate period $T_u$ (e.g., $K_u \approx 7$, $T_u \approx 0.5\text{ s}$).
* **Why the disk stops short at low $K_p$:** Near the setpoint, small position error yields a small `command` whose resulting torque cannot overcome static friction (stiction).
* **What integral action changes:** Adding an integral term ($K_i \int e\,dt$) accumulates steady error over time to eliminate steady-state offset, at the cost of extra phase lag that can reduce stability margin if $K_i$ is too high.

## 10. Verification

* **What `tb_disk_top` checks vs. does not check:** It verifies digital integration and closed-loop behavior—step changes in setpoint, position tracking, direction/PWM responses, and absence of runaway—but does **not** capture real physical friction/stiction, motor torque/voltage sag, or exact hardware settling times.
* **Testbench catching a backwards decoder:** `tb_quad_decoder` directly drives known quadrature sequences (`A`, `B`) in both directions and checks `position` increments/decrements (`tb_disk_top` would also reflect inverted loop direction).
* **Why the safety gate requires `tb_disk_top` before arming:** Passing `tb_disk_top` confirms core datapaths and control/drive wiring behave as intended in simulation before applying power to the physical motor rig, reducing the chance of unexpected motion or runaway.
